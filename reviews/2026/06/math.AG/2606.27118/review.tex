\section*{TL;DR}
The five specialist reviews converge on a paper that makes genuine incremental contributions to the classification of automorphism groups of uniform dessins d'enfants with genus \ensuremath{\ge} 2, directly extending the author's prior work (Ohnishi26). The novelty specialist rates the work incremental (score 0.45, confidence 0.68): the new [b\^{}q,b\^{}q,n] theorem, two counterexamples to conjectures in Ohnishi26, and the [n,n,n] classification are real advances over the prior art, though the scope is narrow and the Lando--Zvonkin monograph and Jones's primitive-groups classification results are absent from the literature survey. The technical correctness specialist rates overall correctness as mostly\_sound (confidence 0.70) but flags four major-severity claims (C2, C8, C9, C11) as unsupported by executable evidence: the b=3 explicit construction (prop:c3b3), the [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] counterexample, the [15,5\^{}3,5\^{}3] cardinality claims, and the b=2 alternative-proof construction. The reproducibility specialist (confidence 0.86, score 0.22) independently designates the missing [b\^{}q,b\^{}q,n] proof artifact and the [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] enumeration as critical-severity concerns, satisfying the recommendation gate for math.AG: at least two headline-adjacent missing proof-as-code artifacts are flagged at critical severity, defaulting the recommendation to major\_revision. The hand proofs for thm:trivautd (C6) and thm:class1 (C7) are judged fully supported, so the qualitative conclusions of the paper are plausible; the deficit is the absence of any computational corroboration for the explicit constructions and finite enumerations on which several headline results rest. Citation issues are moderate: two bibliographic errors (Horie24 DOI prefix mismatch, Goupil98 arXiv/DOI confusion), one redundant preprint--journal pair (Hidalgo18/19), the absence of Grothendieck's Esquisse d'un Programme despite its explicit mention in the introduction, and the unusual choice of a Master's thesis for a textbook-level result.

\textbf{Recommendation:} Major revision \quad \textbf{Confidence:} 78\%\\

\section*{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The theorem that every uniform passport [b\^{}q,b\^{}q,n] of genus \ensuremath{\ge} 2 admits a dessin with trivial automorphism group is a novel, complete result for a previously unresolved passport class, proved via a well-structured case analysis with an independently verified group-theoretic skeleton.
  \item The counterexamples to two conjectures from Ohnishi26---showing [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] admits no trivial-Aut dessin and [5\^{}3,5\^{}3,15] admits only trivial-Aut dessins---demonstrate genuine discovery rather than routine extension of prior techniques.
  \item Theorem thm:class1 on [n,n,n] passports, constructing dessins with |Aut D| equal to any prescribed divisor r of n, is clean, fully self-contained, and receives a complete verification in the technical-correctness review with no gaps identified.
  \item Theorem thm:trivautd (all dessins with passport [ℓ₁ℓ₂, ℓ₂\^{}ℓ₁, ℓ₂\^{}ℓ₁] have trivial automorphism group for distinct primes) is fully proved by a self-contained block-system argument that the technical-correctness specialist finds sound.
  \item The counting framework---Goupil-based lower bound |N| and centralizer-based upper bound |D|---is systematically developed and provides a reusable analytic tool for future work on dessin enumeration.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The headline counterexample that [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] admits no dessin with trivial automorphism group (C8) is stated purely 'by direct computation' with no executable code, GAP script, or verifiable enumeration artifact, making it impossible to independently confirm the claimed counts of 920 permutations across 19 conjugacy classes.
  \item The explicit permutation construction in prop:c3b3 (b=3 subcase of the main theorem) involves intricate modular index arithmetic and a q mod 4 case split that is verified only by spot-check examples, not by a general computational certificate (C2).
  \item The numerical claims for the [15,5\^{}3,5\^{}3] passport---|N(5,3,5)|=4354560, 290304 conjugacy classes of size 15---are asserted as direct computation results without any accompanying code or enumeration log (C9).
  \item The alternative-proof construction for the [n,b\^{}q,n] b=2 subcase (prop:c2b2) replicates the same unverified explicit-construction pattern as C2 and C11: mechanically checkable assertions backed only by worked examples.
  \item No code repository, scripts, or computational environment specification is provided, making independent reproduction of any permutation-group centralizer calculation or example search impossible.
  \item Two bibliography entries contain factual errors: the DOI for Horie24 resolves to a Springer journal of mathematical imaging rather than the stated Mathematical Journal of Okayama University, and Goupil98 records a DOI suffix as an arXiv identifier.
  \item Grothendieck's Esquisse d'un Programme, credited in the introduction as the conceptual origin of the dessin d'enfants program, is absent from the references, a notable omission given its centrality to the field.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Open Questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Can the authors provide a GAP, SageMath, or Magma script that enumerates all permutations y giving the [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] passport and confirms that none yields trivial automorphism group, reproducing the claimed counts of 920 permutations and 19 conjugacy classes?
  \item For prop:c3b3 (b=3 subcase), can the intricate modular index arithmetic in the construction of y be verified computationally for all odd q up to, say, q=101, with the cycle-type and centrality assertions confirmed?
  \item Can the authors clarify or correct the Horie24 bibliographic entry, specifically whether the DOI 10.1007/s10851-023-01159-6 is correct for an article in the Mathematical Journal of Okayama University?
  \item What is the reason Grothendieck's Esquisse d'un Programme is not cited, given that the introduction explicitly credits Grothendieck's fascination with Belyi's theorem as the origin of the dessin d'enfants theory?
  \item Do the open cases in the updated automorphism-group distribution table correspond to genuine structural obstructions, or are they simply cases where the counting argument's boundary conditions are not yet sharp enough to resolve the question?
  \item The novelty specialist notes that Jones's classification of primitive permutation groups containing an n-cycle is not cited despite its relevance to monodromy-group primitivity; is this omission intentional, and how does the paper's treatment of primitive groups relate to that classification?
\end{itemize}
