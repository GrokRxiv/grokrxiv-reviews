\section*{TL;DR}
This paper extends Ohnishi26 by characterizing automorphism groups of uniform dessins d'enfants with passports [b\^{}q,b\^{}q,n] and [n,n,n], presenting counterexamples to previously stated conjectures and an alternative combinatorial proof for the [n,b\^{}q,n] case. Specialists are broadly aligned: the technical-correctness reviewer assessed the paper as mostly\_sound (confidence 0.62) with three major-severity gaps in unverified computational claims (C3, C9, C11), the reproducibility reviewer found no code or proof artifacts and assigned a score of 0.28 with one critical-severity gap in the headline theorem, the novelty reviewer scored the contribution as incremental (0.45) given the close dependence on Ohnishi26, and the citation reviewer identified two bibliographic data errors and the absence of Grothendieck's foundational Esquisse. No substantive disagreement exists between specialists; the convergent assessment is that the analytical results are largely sound but load-bearing computational claims are unverifiable without shipped enumeration scripts or formal proof certificates. Because the field is code-amenable (math.AG) and the reproducibility reviewer flagged a critical-severity missing proof artifact for the headline theorem, the recommendation gate applies and the recommendation is major\_revision.

\textbf{Recommendation:} Major revision \quad \textbf{Confidence:} 78\%\\

\section*{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The theorem characterizing [n,n,n] passports of genus at least 2 (Thm thm:class1) is proved by an explicit, symbolic construction with a complete centralizer argument and is fully self-contained.
  \item Theorem thm:trivautd for passports [l1*l2, l2\^{}l1, l2\^{}l1] with l1 < l2 distinct odd primes is rigorous and self-contained, using only block-system arguments that do not rely on external computation.
  \item The b=2 subcase (Prop prop:c2b2) is constructively proved with an explicit permutation pair, cycle-type verification by element coverage, and a mutually exclusive commutator constraint argument confirmed on worked cases q=4,6,8,10.
  \item The b=3 subcase (Prop prop:c3b3) is proved symbolically via an explicit cycle table, a covering argument for all q mod 4 residues, and a direct centralizer check on two test edges, with consistency confirmed for q=3,5,7,9.
  \item The Goupil-Schaeffer lower bound (Lemma lem:Nbqblower) and digamma-based upper bound (Lemma lem:Dupper) provide a principled asymptotic counting framework that cleanly handles the generic b>=5 branch of the headline theorem.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Three load-bearing claims---that [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4] admits no dessin with trivial automorphism group (C3), that the boundary-case (|N|,|D|) values in Table tab:exceptionbq and the corresponding explicit y entries have trivial centralizers (C9), and that [12\^{}2,2\^{}12,6\^{}4] falsifies the [(4t)\^{}2,2\^{}\{4t\},(2t)\^{}4] family conjecture (C11)---are each asserted from 'direct computation' with no shipped enumeration script, certificate, or witness permutation, making them independently unverifiable in this code-amenable field.
  \item The b>=5 branch of the headline theorem (Thm thm:class3) depends on monotonicity and numeric evaluations of R(b,q) at boundary pairs (e.g., R(5,3)>1, R(5,q) increasing in q) that are asserted but not confirmed by any executable artifact; no formal proof file (e.g., Proofs/Class3.lean) or verification script is provided, which the reproducibility reviewer flagged at critical severity.
  \item Multiple foundational results invoked throughout---including Lemma 2.7, Proposition 2.10, and Theorem 7.6---originate in Ohnishi26, an unreviewed arXiv preprint by the same author, creating a chain-of-trust gap that is not acknowledged in the paper.
  \item Two bibliographic data errors require correction: the Horie24 entry carries a DOI with the Springer prefix s10851 (belonging to the Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision, not the Mathematical Journal of Okayama University), and Goupil98 carries '1998.0215' as an arXiv identifier that is actually the DOI suffix.
  \item Grothendieck's Esquisse d'un Programme is not cited despite the introduction explicitly crediting Grothendieck with founding the theory of dessins d'enfants, and the Schneps edited volume (LMS Lecture Note Series 200, 1994) is absent despite the paper discussing Galois orbits and the absolute Galois group acting on dessins.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Open Questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item For the counterexample [8\^{}2,2\^{}8,4\^{}4]: can the authors provide an enumeration script (GAP or Sage) that fixes x=(1..8)(9..16), enumerates all y of cycle type (2\^{}8) with (xy)\^{}\{-1\} of type (4\^{}4), and reproduces the 920-permutation, 19-conjugacy-class breakdown with the automorphism group order distribution (11/6/1/1 classes of orders 2/4/8/16)?
  \item For the family-conjecture falsification: can an explicit pair (x,y) for [12\^{}2,2\^{}12,6\^{}4] be stated in the paper (or a companion script provided) certifying that the centralizer of the generated group in Sym(24) is trivial?
  \item For the boundary cases in Table tab:exceptionbq: can a script be provided that computes |N(b,q,b)| and |D(n)| exactly for (b,q)=(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(4,2) and verifies that each listed explicit y has trivial centralizer, and also evaluates R(b,q) at (3,5),(4,4),(5,2) to confirm these exceed 1?
  \item Several results here (Lemma 2.7, Prop 2.10, Thm 7.6) rest on Ohnishi26, which has not completed peer review; how do the authors assess the robustness of the current paper's conclusions if any result in Ohnishi26 is revised or retracted?
  \item The Wakabayashi22 reference concerns Belyi's theorem in positive characteristic, whereas this paper works entirely over number fields; can the authors identify the specific result in the present paper that this reference supports, or consider removing it?
\end{itemize}
